# PR 10 work log: free-text what-ifs

Branch `sa/layer-b-whatif`. Typed what-ifs go through compile, research and
interviews, and come back to the page with a confidence tier.

## The Harding disclosure and the manipulation check

- **What went wrong.** "Harding came out as gay" first ran with the news in
  the brief's settled facts, naming the nominee only by his party's
  descriptor. Most of the 12 people interviewed pinned the news on the other
  candidate, and the interviews moved 7.8 points *toward* Harding
  (DISCLOSURES D21). The published run, `1920-3c7b694c10`, has Harding at
  404–127 and the voters leaning 1.2 points toward him.
- **The fix (prompt p5, compiler c3).**
  - News about one nominee is printed on that nominee's own ballot line
    (`about`, `nominee_news`). The descriptor-only news path is gone.
  - Every such counterfactual also answers `news_about`: whose news it was.
    Anyone who names the other candidate is left out of the counts, kept in
    the interviews with `misread: true`, and counted in the confidence
    flags (`misread-change`).
  - Half or more misreading forces "extremely low"; more than a quarter
    costs one step.
- **The rerun** (`1920-1a2dcd24ad`): 0 of 12 misread. The voters lean 12.6
  points toward Cox, Indiana, Kansas, Nebraska, New Hampshire and two more
  flip, and Harding still wins, 331–200, in 100 of 100 draws. The tier stays
  "extremely low": the change is far from anything on record.
- **Post-hoc.** The spec was recompiled with c3 after the first result was
  seen. Its key, label and words were kept.
